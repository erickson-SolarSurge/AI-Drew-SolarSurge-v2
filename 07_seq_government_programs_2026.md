## Solar Sharer Offer — Full Explainer

The Solar Sharer Offer (SSO) is a **brand-new regulated electricity offering** that launched on **1 July 2026**. It is the biggest new program to talk about on SEQ calls.

### What It Is
The SSO is an opt-in regulated standing offer under the **Australian Energy Regulator's Default Market Offer (DMO)** framework. It gives eligible households **3 hours of completely free electricity** during the middle of the day, when national solar generation is at its peak.

### Key Details
- **Free electricity window:** 11am to 2pm AEST in South East Queensland
- **Who can access it:** Any SEQ residential customer with a **smart (advanced digital) meter**
- **Solar required?** No — households without solar panels can access this offer
- **Opt-in:** Customers must sign up through their energy retailer. It is NOT automatic.
- **Retailer requirement:** All retailers with 1,000+ customers in SEQ are required to offer it
- **Available in:** NSW, South Australia, and South East Queensland (not regional QLD — see below)

### Regional QLD Note
A separate Solar Sharer Offer for regional Queensland (Ergon network) was also approved by the QCA from 1 July 2026, with equivalent structure and rates to the SEQ offer.

### How to Qualify for It
1. Must be a residential customer in the SEQ/Energex network area
2. Must have a smart meter (advanced digital meter) installed
3. Must sign up through their energy retailer

### Why It Matters for Solar + Battery Customers
The SSO is even more powerful when combined with solar and battery:
- During the free 11am–2pm window, the home draws free grid electricity
- Solar generation during this same period can be directed entirely into the battery
- The battery is then fully charged with free energy to use in the evening
- Result: Free grid power during the day + Free stored solar power in the evening = near-zero grid dependence

## Understanding SEQ's Feed-in Tariff Landscape

The SEQ feed-in tariff is **deregulated**. There is no mandated minimum rate on the Energex network. Retailers set their own rates.

**Current range:** Approximately **3–10¢/kWh** depending on the retailer and plan the customer is on.

### The Trajectory That Matters
| Period | Feed-in Tariff |
|---|---|
| Solar Bonus Scheme era (pre-2012) | 44¢/kWh |
| 2015–2018 | ~10–15¢/kWh |
| 2020–2022 | ~8–12¢/kWh |
| 2024–2026 | ~3–10¢/kWh |

The trend is clear and consistent: **FiTs are declining, not recovering.** This matters because:

- Homeowners who went solar 5–10 years ago hoping to "earn money" from their panels are increasingly disillusioned
- The Export Trap is wider than ever — sell at 3c, buy back at 30c
- This frustration is a powerful opening for the battery retrofit conversation (Energy Upgrade)

### How to Use FiT Frustration on Calls

> **Prospect:** "I've had solar for years but the feed-in tariff has dropped so much it's barely worth it."

> **Setter:** "Yeah, that's a really common frustration and I completely understand. Feed-in tariffs in South East Queensland have gone from 44 cents years ago to basically 3 to 10 cents now depending on your retailer. And that gap is going to keep widening. The fix isn't more panels — it's storage. A battery means instead of exporting for 3 cents, you keep that energy and use it tonight. We'd love to show you the numbers on a free audit — you might be surprised how much you're losing to the grid right now."

## The Legacy Solar Bonus Scheme (44¢)

Some long-term solar owners in SEQ are still receiving the **Solar Bonus Scheme rate of 44¢/kWh**. Key facts:
- This rate is no longer available to new customers
- Existing recipients who maintain eligibility will continue receiving it until **1 July 2028**, when it expires permanently
- Customers on the 44¢ tariff must be careful — adding a battery may affect their eligibility depending on their system configuration. Direct any such queries to the consultant.
- If a prospect mentions they receive the 44¢ rate, flag this clearly in the appointment notes.

## Knowledge Check

**Q1: What is the Solar Sharer Offer and when did it launch?**  
A: A regulated opt-in electricity tariff launched 1 July 2026 that provides 3 hours of free electricity from 11am–2pm AEST in SEQ. No solar required — any residential customer with a smart meter can access it.

**Q2: Does the Solar Sharer Offer require the household to have solar panels?**  
A: No. Any SEQ residential customer with a smart meter can opt in, with or without solar.

**Q3: How does a customer access the Solar Sharer Offer?**  
A: They opt in through their energy retailer. It is not automatic.

**Q4: What is the current feed-in tariff range for SEQ solar owners?**  
A: Approximately 3–10¢/kWh — deregulated on the Energex network, varies by retailer.

**Q5: A prospect mentions they are currently on the 44¢/kWh Solar Bonus Scheme. What should you do?**  
A: Flag this clearly in the appointment notes for the consultant. Adding a battery may affect their eligibility, and the 44¢ rate expires on 1 July 2028. The consultant needs to assess their specific situation.

**Q6: Why does declining feed-in tariff actually help the battery sales conversation?**  
A: Lower FiT = Export Trap is worse than ever = battery saves more money. "You're sending your power to the grid for 3 cents and buying it back at 30 cents. Storage is the only fix."
