## Federal Cheaper Home Batteries Program — Full Explainer

This is the **primary incentive to mention on QLD calls**. It is a federal government program administered through the Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme (SRES).

### How It Works
- Creates Small-scale Technology Certificates (STCs) that reduce the upfront purchase price of an eligible battery system
- Applied at **point-of-sale** by the installer — the customer receives the discount immediately. No government form to fill, no waiting for a cheque.
- Applies to batteries with nominal capacity between 5kWh and 100kWh
- Works on batteries added to **new OR existing solar systems**
- No means test — any eligible homeowner or small business qualifies
- Program is funded to **$7.2 billion** and runs until **2030**

### Current Rebate Value (May–December 2026)
- Approximately **$252–$272 per usable kWh** for the first 14kWh of battery capacity
- A typical **10kWh battery**: approximately **$2,520–$2,720 off** the upfront cost
- STC factor is currently 6.8 (from May 2026)
- Rebate steps down every **6 months** — in January and July
- Program was expanded from $2.3B to $7.2B in December 2025

### What Changed on 1 May 2026
- The program restructured to a **tiered structure** — the rebate applies to the first 14kWh of usable capacity
- Step-downs now occur **twice yearly** (January and July) rather than annually
- This means the same battery installed later in the year will attract a smaller rebate

### On-Call Framing
> Do NOT quote exact dollar figures on calls. Use:  
> "Around 30% off" or "typically a few thousand dollars off depending on the system size."  
> Let the consultant give the precise figure at the audit.

## Federal STC Solar Rebate (SRES) — Explained

The Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme (SRES) also covers solar panels — not just batteries. This is how the solar "rebate" works:

- The rebate is applied **upfront at the point of sale** by the installer
- Queensland is mostly in STC **Zone 1 and Zone 2** (high solar resource = higher STC value)
- For 2026, the deeming period is **5 years**
- The rebate reduces each year until the SRES ends in 2030
- The system must be installed by an accredited installer using eligible equipment

**On-call framing:** "The federal solar rebate is already factored into any quote we give you — it's applied upfront, so you don't pay the full sticker price and then claim it back. Our consultant will show you exactly what the system costs after the rebate."

## Feed-in Tariff (FiT) Landscape — Queensland

### SEQ (Energex network — Brisbane, Gold Coast, Sunshine Coast, Ipswich, Logan)
- **Deregulated** — no mandated minimum rate
- Current market rate: approximately **3–10¢/kWh** depending on retailer and plan
- Homeowners can shop around for the best FiT, but rates are generally low

### Regional Queensland (Ergon Energy network)
- **Regulated** — minimum rate set by the Queensland Competition Authority (QCA)
- Current regulated minimum: **8.66¢/kWh** (set annually from 1 July)
- More favourable than SEQ but still well below historical rates

### The Legacy Solar Bonus Scheme (44¢/kWh)
- This was the original Queensland feed-in tariff — 44 cents per kWh
- **No longer available to new customers**
- Only existing recipients can continue to receive it — expires 1 July 2028
- If a prospect mentions the "44 cent tariff," clarify: it's a legacy rate and only active if they were already receiving it before the scheme closed

## Supercharged Solar for Renters

A new QLD government initiative launched in **December 2025**:
- Up to **$3,500 rebate for Queensland landlords** who install solar on rental properties
- Target: improve access to solar for renters who cannot install their own systems
- Relevant when speaking to customers who identify as landlords with investment properties

## Knowledge Check

**Q1: A prospect asks about the QLD Battery Booster. What do you tell them?**  
A: The QLD Battery Booster closed to new applications in May 2024. Redirect them to the Federal Cheaper Home Batteries Program, which offers approximately 30% off and runs until 2030.

**Q2: Is the Federal Cheaper Home Batteries Program means-tested?**  
A: No — any eligible homeowner or small business qualifies regardless of income.

**Q3: How often does the federal battery rebate step down?**  
A: Every 6 months — in January and July. This creates genuine urgency.

**Q4: What is the approximate current federal battery rebate for a 10kWh battery in QLD?**  
A: Approximately $2,520–$2,720 off the upfront cost (do not quote this on calls — frame as "around 30% off" and let the consultant confirm the exact figure).

**Q5: What is the current regulated feed-in tariff for Regional QLD (Ergon network)?**  
A: 8.66¢/kWh — set by the QCA annually from 1 July. SEQ is deregulated at 3–10¢/kWh.

**Q6: What is the Supercharged Solar for Renters program?**  
A: Up to $3,500 for QLD landlords to install solar on rental properties. Launched December 2025. Relevant for prospects who identify as landlords.
