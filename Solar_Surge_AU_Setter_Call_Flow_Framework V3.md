# Solar Surge AU — Outbound Setter Call Flow & Script Framework
**Source:** Live call analysis — Setter: Luca Gye | Date: October 12, 2026
**Purpose:** AI LMS reference — standardised outbound call flow for all appointment setters

## How to Use This Document

This framework was reverse-engineered from a real outbound setter call. Each phase includes:
- **What the setter did** (observed behaviour)
- **The script template** (standardised language for all setters)
- **The goal of the phase** (why it matters)
- **Coaching notes** (what to watch for)

Work through each phase in order. Do not skip phases or reorder them.

## Phase 1: Opener & Identity

### What Happened on the Call
Luca answered the outbound ring and immediately introduced himself, his name ("Luke"), and the company (Solar Surge Australia), then anchored credibility by referencing a prior call the lead had with the call centre team.

### Script Template

> **"Hi, is this [Lead First Name]? Great — my name's [Your Name], calling from Solar Surge Australia. You would have spoken to our call centre team [recently / over the weekend / last Friday] — does that ring a bell for you?"**

### Goal
- Confirm you have the right person
- Establish legitimacy by referencing the prior contact
- Reduce call resistance immediately ("we're following up, not cold calling")

### Coaching Notes
- **Always confirm the lead's name first** before launching into your intro. Luca called the lead "Glenn" when the lead's name was actually "Whitney" — this is a credibility hit. Read the name off the CRM carefully.
- Keep the opener short. One breath. Don't over-explain at this stage.

## Phase 2: Context Bridge (Notes Recap)

### What Happened on the Call
Luca used notes left by the call centre to recap what the lead had previously said — solar age (~15 years), power bill (~$600/quarter) — and asked the lead to confirm accuracy.

### Script Template

> **"The notes our team left behind mentioned a few things — just want to get us on the same page. It says you've got solar up on the roof, about [X] years old, and you're paying around [$X] on your power bills every [quarter / month]. Does that all sound about right?"**

### Goal
- Signal that you've done your homework (builds trust)
- Get the lead saying "yes" early (micro-commitments)
- Confirm or correct CRM data before proceeding

### Coaching Notes
- If the lead corrects the data, update it live and thank them. Don't dismiss corrections.
- The address confirmation ("We have 27 Pindari Street on file — is that right?") should follow immediately after the bill confirmation. Always verify the address on this call.

**Address Confirm Script:**
> **"And just double-checking — we have [address] on file. Is that still the right property?"**

## Phase 3: Qualification Questions

### What Happened on the Call
Luca asked a series of qualifying questions woven naturally into conversation rather than as a rigid checklist. Questions covered: system origin (installed or pre-existing), ownership, age of lead, property sell plans, roof damage, and decision-maker presence.

### The Six Qualifiers — Script & Sequence

**Q1 — System Origin**
> **"Did you guys install that system yourselves, or was it already up there when you bought the property?"**

*Why it matters:* Older DIY or neighbour-installed systems are often underperforming, creating a stronger upgrade case.

---

**Q2 — Decision-Maker / Ownership**
> **"And are you the sole owner of the property, or do you own it with a partner or family member?"**

*Follow-up if partner mentioned:*
> **"Would [she/he/they] be home at the same time as you? We'd likely have a couple of questions for both of you."**

*Why it matters:* Both decision-makers must be present at the appointment. Missing a co-owner = wasted field rep visit.

---

**Q3 — Age Qualifier (Pension / Finance Eligibility)**
> **"Just a quick one — since you mentioned you're on the pension, I do have to ask: are you over or under the age of 65?"**

*If over 65:*
> **"And roughly what age, if you don't mind me asking? Just want to make sure the government incentive side works for your situation — I believe the cutoff is around 75, so you should be fine."**

*Why it matters:* Finance and government incentive eligibility has age restrictions. Flag early; don't let field reps discover this at the appointment.

---

**Q4 — Property Sell Intent**
> **"Are there any plans to sell the property in the near future?"**

*Why it matters:* If they're selling within 12 months, the system ROI case changes significantly. Note on file — don't disqualify outright, but inform the field rep.

---

**Q5 — Roof Condition / Damage**
> **"With the storms / hail that came through [recently / last year], did you guys get any damage to the roof at all?"**

*Why it matters:* Roof damage affects panel placement and may need to be resolved before install. Field rep needs to know.

---

**Q6 — Solar Panel Location**
> **"And the panels — are they on the main roof, or somewhere else on the property like a shed or garage?"**

*Why it matters:* Panels on sheds or garages affect shading analysis, inverter placement, and system design. Flag for field rep.

## Phase 4: Value Proposition & Pitch

### What Happened on the Call
After qualifying, Luca delivered the free solar assessment pitch — framing it as marketing for the company (low pressure), describing the scope of the visit (roof, system, property, meter box, power bills), and giving a time estimate (45 min – 1 hour).

### Script Template

> **"So the reason we'd be giving you a call is that we've actually got some of our guys out in [your area / the area] at the moment. While we're out there, what we do for people is a free solar assessment — it's basically a bit of marketing for us. Our rep comes out, takes a look at the system on the roof, sees how it's all performing, assesses the property to see what the possibility of more panels or a battery could look like — where it could all fit — and then goes through your power bills, switchboard, and meter box. Based on all of that, they can tell you exactly what a financial upgrade would look like for your situation and give you a full picture of where everything would go. The whole thing takes about 45 minutes — up to an hour if you've got a lot of questions."**

### Goal
- Frame the visit as low-pressure and free
- Make the lead feel like they're getting expert insight, not a sales pitch
- Anchor the time commitment (45–60 min) so there are no surprises

### Coaching Notes
- Lead with "free" and "marketing for us" — this significantly reduces resistance.
- Mention the specific things the rep will look at (roof, system, power bills, switchboard, meter box). Specificity builds credibility.

## Phase 5: Objection Handling

### What Happened on the Call
The lead raised a cost concern — pension income, worried about $12,000 battery costs. Luca redirected to the no-out-of-pocket government incentive structure.

### Objection: "I can't afford it / I'm on the pension"

**Script:**

> **"That's completely fair — and honestly, that's exactly why this is worth a look. There are no out-of-pocket cost options available through government incentives — most people aren't aware of how they're structured. What we aim to do is wipe out your power bill with a proper solar system, and then we use that same amount you're already spending on power — say, your [$X] a quarter — as the payment for the system. So instead of paying the power company, you're putting that money toward owning the system. No extra cost out of your pocket."**

*Then bridge back to the appointment:*
> **"It's just a government incentive we show people how to take advantage of. That's part of what our guy would walk you through when he comes out."**

---

### Objection: "I'm not sure if I qualify / age concern"

**Script:**

> **"Good question — and I want to make sure we get that right for you. I believe the cutoff is around 75, so at [age], you should be fine. What I'd suggest is we get one of our guys out there so they can take a proper look at everything and confirm exactly what you're eligible for. That way you have the full picture."**

---

### Objection: "We're thinking about selling"

**Script:**

> **"Understood — that's worth knowing. Even if you do sell, a solar system with a battery typically adds value to the property, and buyers factor in the reduced running costs. But let's get our guy out there first so you've got all the information to make the best decision either way."**

---

## Phase 6: Appointment Setting

### What Happened on the Call
Luca asked an either/or availability question (mornings vs afternoons), listened to the lead's specific available days, then offered a specific slot he had available (3PM Tuesday) rather than leaving it open-ended.

### Script Template

**Step 1 — Availability Question (Either/Or)**
> **"When would work best to get something booked in — earlier in the mornings, or later in the afternoons?"**

**Step 2 — Days Question**
> **"And which days generally work best for you?"**

**Step 3 — Offer a Specific Slot**
> **"I've actually got a [time] on [day] available. Would that work for you?"**

**Step 4 — Decision-Maker Confirmation**
> **"And [partner's name / your partner] — would they be home at that time as well? We'd probably have a couple of questions for both of you."**

### Goal
- Use either/or questions to guide, not overwhelm
- Offer a specific slot — don't make the lead do the scheduling work
- Lock in both decision-makers before confirming

### Coaching Notes
- Never leave the time slot open-ended ("when are you free?"). Always offer a specific time after getting availability direction.
- If a lead says afternoons only, check your afternoon slots first and pitch one. Don't read out all available times.

---

## Phase 7: Confirm & Close

### What Happened on the Call
Luca recapped all key details on file (address, bill amount), added contextual notes (bill currently lower due to fewer people, but expected to rise with summer and AC use), asked the lead to gather bills before the appointment, and captured the energy provider.

### Script Template

**Address & Bill Confirm:**
> **"Let me just double-check everything. So we've got [address] on file, bill around [$X] a quarter — does that all still sound right?"**

**Contextual Notes (capture any bill caveats):**
> **"And just so our rep has the full picture — is that bill a fairly typical one for you, or has it been higher or lower than usual lately?"**

*Note any reasons for variance (fewer people in house, seasonal change, AC usage, etc.) and add to CRM notes.*

**Request Bills:**
> **"If you can, try and dig up a power bill or two before [rep name] comes out — even just on your phone. That'll help them give you the most accurate picture."**

**Energy Provider:**
> **"One more thing — do you know who your current energy provider is? I'll grab that for the file."**

**Closing Line:**
> **"Perfect. So we've got you locked in for [time] on [day]. Our rep will be out to take a look at everything and give you a full breakdown. If any questions come to mind before then, feel free to jot them down — they'll be able to answer everything on the day. Look forward to seeing you then!"**

## Coaching Notes & Common Mistakes (From This Call)

### ✅ What Luca Did Well
- Used the notes-recap bridge confidently and got early buy-in
- Handled the pension/cost objection smoothly with the no-out-of-pocket frame
- Asked for both decision-makers to be present
- Offered a specific appointment slot rather than asking open-ended availability
- Captured the energy provider at the end
- Added context to the CRM note (bill lower recently, will rise with summer/AC)
- Kept rapport warm and natural throughout — didn't sound scripted

### ⚠️ Areas for Improvement

**1. Lead Name Error**
Luca called the lead "Glenn" when the lead's name (Whitney) was on file. This is a confidence-killer with the lead.
- **Fix:** Read the name off the CRM before dialling. Confirm it on the call opener: *"Is this Whitney?"*

**2. Age Qualification — Uncertainty**
Luca said *"I think it's under 75... I'm not a thousand percent sure."* Expressing uncertainty about eligibility rules undermines trust.
- **Fix:** Know the age eligibility rule before every call. If uncertain, reframe: *"Our rep will confirm the exact eligibility on the day — I just want to make sure we're in the right ballpark, which we are."*

**3. Decision-Maker Clarity**
There was some confusion mid-call about whether the wife was the property owner vs the lead. This should be clarified cleanly.
- **Fix:** If ownership seems shared, ask directly: *"And is the property in both names, or one name? Just so we make sure the right person's on the appointment."*

**4. Panel Location Discovered Late**
The panels being on the shed/garage (not the main roof) came up late in the call. This affects the field rep's preparation.
- **Fix:** Move the "where are the panels located?" question earlier — into the qualifier block, right after confirming system age.

## Quick Reference — Call Flow at a Glance

```
1. OPENER          → Confirm name + reference prior call
2. CONTEXT BRIDGE  → Recap CRM notes, confirm address & bill
3. QUALIFY         → System origin → Ownership → Age → Sell intent → Roof → Panel location
4. VALUE PROP      → Free assessment pitch (45–60 min, low pressure)
5. OBJECTIONS      → Cost / pension → Age eligibility → Sell intent
6. APPT SETTING    → Either/or availability → Offer specific slot → Confirm both DMs present
7. CONFIRM & CLOSE → Recap address + bill → Request bills → Get energy provider → Close
```
